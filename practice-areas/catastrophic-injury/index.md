# Atlanta Catastrophic Injury Lawyer

Some injuries change everything: work, family, home, the future. A catastrophic injury case has to account for all of it, including care you'll need years from now.

## Georgia at a glance

- **Lifetime**: future medical care and lost earning power are part of the claim
- **Liens**: health insurers and hospitals may claim part of a recovery; we deal with them
- **2 years**: to file most injury lawsuits in Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)

## What counts as a catastrophic injury

- Traumatic brain injuries
- Spinal cord injuries and paralysis
- Amputations
- Severe burns
- Injuries that leave permanent disability or disfigurement

## Building a case around the rest of your life

The bills you have today are only part of it. A full claim looks at future surgeries and therapy, home changes, equipment, in-home care, and the income you can no longer earn. We work with your doctors and, when needed, outside experts to put real numbers on those needs.

> **Why medical bills matter so much.** In serious injury cases, health insurers, hospitals and government programs often have a right to be paid back from the recovery. ### What changed in Georgia injury law in 2025?

Georgia passed a major tort reform law, Senate Bill 68, signed on April 21, 2025. For injuries on or after that date, the defense may show the jury whether you wore a seat belt, and what was actually paid, or is needed to pay, your medical bills, not just what was billed. It also limits how lawyers can suggest dollar amounts for pain and suffering, and in most cases lets either side require a trial to be split into a fault phase and a damages phase. These rules make careful handling of medical bills and evidence more important than ever.
