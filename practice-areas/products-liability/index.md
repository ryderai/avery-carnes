# Atlanta Product Liability Lawyer

When a product fails the way it was never supposed to, the company that made it can be responsible. Keep the product, and call us before anyone else asks for it.

## Georgia at a glance

- **O.C.G.A. § 51-1-11**: Georgia's product liability law, including strict liability for manufacturers
- **10 years**: the general cut-off for strict liability claims, counted from the product's first sale
- **Keep it**: the product itself is often the most important piece of evidence

## Kinds of product cases

- **Design defects:** the product is dangerous as designed.
- **Manufacturing defects:** something went wrong when this one was made.
- **Warning defects:** the company didn't warn about a danger it knew about.

## How Georgia law works

Under O.C.G.A. § 51-1-11, a manufacturer can be held strictly liable when a product that was not merchantable and reasonably suited to its use causes injury. That means you may not have to prove the company was careless, only that the product was defective and caused the harm.

Georgia also has a statute of repose: strict liability claims generally must be brought within 10 years of the product's first sale. Some negligence claims can be brought later, including claims that a manufacturer failed to warn about a danger it learned of, and claims about products that cause disease or birth defects. Timing matters, so call early.
