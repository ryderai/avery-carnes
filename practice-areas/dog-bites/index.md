# Atlanta Dog Bite Lawyer

A serious bite can mean surgery, scars and fear that lasts. Georgia law can hold a careless owner responsible, and homeowner's or renter's insurance often pays these claims.

## Georgia at a glance

- **O.C.G.A. § 51-2-7**: Georgia's dog bite law
- **Leash laws**: breaking a local leash law can help show an owner is responsible
- **2 years**: to file most injury lawsuits in Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)

## When is a dog owner responsible in Georgia?

Under O.C.G.A. § 51-2-7, an owner can be liable when the dog is vicious or dangerous and the owner carelessly manages it or lets it go at large, and the person bitten did not provoke it. If the dog was off its leash where a local law required one, that can help show the dog was dangerous.

## What to do after a dog bite

1. Get medical care. Bites can get infected, and a doctor's record matters.
2. Get the owner's name, address and insurance, and the dog's vaccination records.
3. Report the bite to local animal control.
4. Take photos of the injury as it heals, and of where the bite happened.
5. Call us before you talk to the owner's insurance company.

## Who pays

Many dog bite claims are paid by the owner's homeowner's or renter's insurance, not out of the owner's pocket. We find the policy and deal with the insurer.

### How long do I have to file an injury claim in Georgia?

For most injury claims, Georgia gives you **two years from the date of the injury** to file a lawsuit (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Claims against a government are much shorter: written notice to a city is due within **six months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5), and to a county or the State within **twelve months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 and § 50-21-26). Miss the deadline and the claim can be lost for good.
