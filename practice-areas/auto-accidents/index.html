What to do after a car accident in Georgia

  1. Get medical care. Some injuries, like concussions and back injuries, show up hours or days later. A record that starts on day one matters.
  2. Call the police. Georgia law requires reporting a crash that causes injury, death, or at least $500 in property damage (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-273). Ask for the report number.
  3. Take photos and names. Vehicles, the road, traffic lights, your injuries, and the names and phone numbers of witnesses.
  4. Don't give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer before you talk to a lawyer.
  5. Call us. Video from businesses and traffic cameras can be recorded over. The sooner we know, the sooner we can ask for it.

How Georgia law treats your claim

Georgia is an at-fault state. The driver who caused the crash, and their insurance company, are responsible for the harm. You can claim medical bills, lost income, damage to your vehicle, and pain and suffering.

Georgia uses a modified comparative fault rule (O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33). If you were partly at fault, your award goes down by your share. At 50% or more, you recover nothing. That is why insurers work so hard to put some of the blame on you.

Georgia drivers must carry at least $25,000 per person and $50,000 per crash in bodily-injury coverage. Serious injuries often cost more than that. Your own uninsured/underinsured motorist (UM) coverage may pay the difference, so we look at every policy that could apply.

New since 2025

For crashes on or after April 21, 2025, Georgia's tort reform law (SB 68) lets the defense tell the jury whether you wore a seat belt, and show what was actually paid on your medical bills. That makes how your bills and treatment are documented more important than ever. Trey's earlier work for Georgia hospitals on medical bills, liens and subrogation is directly relevant here.

What we do in a car accident case

  • Get the police report, photos, 911 calls, and any camera footage we can find.
  • Find every insurance policy that could pay: the other driver's, their employer's if they were working, and your own UM coverage.
  • Track your medical treatment and bills, and deal with health-insurance and hospital liens so more of the recovery reaches you.
  • Handle every call with the insurers so you can focus on getting better.
  • Prepare the case for trial from the start, so the insurer knows we're ready if a fair offer never comes.

Questions about car accidents cases

Should I give a statement to the other driver's insurance company?

You do not have to give a recorded statement to the other driver's insurer, and it is usually smart to talk to a lawyer first. Their adjuster's job is to pay as little as possible. Your own policy may require you to cooperate with your own insurer, so read it or ask us.

What if I was partly at fault?

Georgia uses a modified comparative fault rule (O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33). You can still recover if you were less than 50% at fault, but your award is reduced by your share of the fault. At 50% or more, you recover nothing. Insurers know this, which is why they often try to shift blame onto the injured person.

How long do I have to file an injury claim in Georgia?

For most injury claims, Georgia gives you two years from the date of the injury to file a lawsuit (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Claims against a government are much shorter: written notice to a city is due within six months (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5), and to a county or the State within twelve months (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 and § 50-21-26). Miss the deadline and the claim can be lost for good.

What is my case worth?

No honest lawyer can give you a number on the first call. What a case is worth depends on the injuries, the medical treatment and bills, lost income, how the injury changes your life, who was at fault, and how much insurance is available. We'll explain each of those for your case and keep you updated as the facts come in.
