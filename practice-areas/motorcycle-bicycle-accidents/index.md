## Georgia at a glance

- **Helmets**: Georgia requires every motorcycle rider and passenger to wear one (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-315)
- **3 feet**: the minimum space a driver must keep when passing a bicycle in the same lane (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-56)
- **Under 50%**: your share of fault must stay below this to recover (O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33)

## Motorcycle crashes

Many motorcycle crashes happen when a driver turns left across a rider's path or changes lanes without seeing them. Afterward, the insurer often argues the rider was speeding or hard to see. Witnesses, scene photos, vehicle damage and camera footage are how we push back.

Georgia requires helmets for every rider and passenger (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-315). If you weren't wearing one, you may still have a claim. Talk to us before you assume otherwise.

## Bicycle crashes

Georgia law requires a driver passing a bicycle to move into a lane not next to the rider when that is practical. If it isn't possible or safe, the driver must slow down (to 25 mph, or 10 mph under the limit, whichever is greater) and keep at least three feet away while passing (O.C.G.A. § 40-6-56). Cyclists also have the right to use the road. When a driver passes too close, opens a door into a bike lane, or turns across a rider, the driver can be responsible.

## Questions

### What if I was partly at fault?

Georgia uses a modified comparative fault rule (O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33). You can still recover if you were less than 50% at fault, but your award is reduced by your share of the fault. At 50% or more, you recover nothing. Insurers know this, which is why they often try to shift blame onto the injured person.

### How long do I have to file an injury claim in Georgia?

For most injury claims, Georgia gives you **two years from the date of the injury** to file a lawsuit (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Claims against a government are much shorter: written notice to a city is due within **six months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5), and to a county or the State within **twelve months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 and § 50-21-26). Miss the deadline and the claim can be lost for good.
