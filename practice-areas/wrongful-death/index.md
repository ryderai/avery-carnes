# Atlanta Wrongful Death Lawyer

Nothing replaces the person you lost. A wrongful death case can get your family answers, hold the responsible people accountable, and ease the money pressure that follows a loss.

## Georgia at a glance

- **2 years**: from the date of death to file in most cases (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)
- **Full value**: of the life of the person who died is what Georgia law measures (O.C.G.A. § 51-4-1 and 51-4-2)
- **2 claims**: a family's wrongful death claim, plus a separate claim by the estate

## Who can bring a wrongful death claim in Georgia

Georgia law sets the order. The surviving spouse brings the claim first and shares it with any children. If there is no spouse, the children bring it. If there is no spouse or child, the parents may. If none of them survive, the administrator of the estate brings it for the next of kin.

## What the claims cover

Georgia measures a wrongful death claim by the **full value of the life** of the person who died, seen from that person's point of view. That includes the income they would have earned and the parts of life that have no price tag.

The estate can bring a separate claim for medical and funeral expenses, and for the pain and suffering the person went through before death.

## Questions

### How long do I have to file an injury claim in Georgia?

For most injury claims, Georgia gives you **two years from the date of the injury** to file a lawsuit (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Claims against a government are much shorter: written notice to a city is due within **six months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5), and to a county or the State within **twelve months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 and § 50-21-26). Miss the deadline and the claim can be lost for good.
