# Atlanta Nursing Home Abuse & Neglect Lawyer

You trusted a facility to care for someone you love. When bedsores, falls, or unexplained injuries show up, you deserve real answers, and the facility should answer for what happened.

## Georgia at a glance

- **O.C.G.A. § 31-8-100**: and following: Georgia's bill of rights for residents of long-term care facilities
- **2 years**: to file most injury lawsuits in Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)
- **911**: if someone is in danger right now, call first and ask questions later

## Warning signs families notice

- Bedsores (pressure ulcers), especially ones that get worse
- Falls, broken bones, or bruises no one can explain
- Sudden weight loss, dehydration, or poor hygiene
- Medication mistakes, or a resident who seems over-sedated
- A loved one who becomes withdrawn or afraid of certain staff

## Your loved one's rights

Georgia law gives residents of long-term care facilities a set of rights, including the right to adequate and appropriate care, and to be free from improper restraints and isolation (O.C.G.A. § 31-8-100 and following, including § 31-8-108 and § 31-8-109). Federal rules also apply to facilities that take Medicare or Medicaid.

## What to do now

1. If anyone is in danger, call 911.
2. Take dated photos and write down what you see, who you spoke to, and when.
3. Ask for your loved one's care records in writing.

## Questions

### How long do I have to file an injury claim in Georgia?

For most injury claims, Georgia gives you **two years from the date of the injury** to file a lawsuit (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Claims against a government are much shorter: written notice to a city is due within **six months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5), and to a county or the State within **twelve months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 and § 50-21-26). Miss the deadline and the claim can be lost for good.
