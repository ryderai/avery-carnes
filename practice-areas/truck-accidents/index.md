# Atlanta Truck Accident Lawyer

A truck crash is not just a bigger car crash. There's a trucking company, its insurer, and federal safety rules, and the company's lawyers often start working the day of the wreck.

## Georgia at a glance

- **49 C.F.R.**: Parts 390–399: the federal safety rules commercial truck companies must follow
- **Part 395**: limits how long a truck driver may drive before resting (hours of service)
- **2 years**: to file most injury lawsuits in Georgia (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33)

## Why truck cases are different

Commercial trucks are governed by federal safety rules (49 C.F.R. Part 382 and Parts 390–399). Those rules cover driver hours, driver qualifications, drug and alcohol testing, and vehicle inspection and maintenance. When a company breaks them, the records usually show it.

More than one party can be responsible: the driver, the trucking company, the company that loaded the cargo, the owner of the trailer, or a maintenance shop. Each may have its own insurance.

## The evidence that matters, and why speed matters

- **Electronic logging device (ELD) data** showing the driver's hours (49 C.F.R. Part 395).
- **Engine and dash camera data** showing speed and braking before the crash.
- **The driver qualification file** and drug and alcohol test results.
- **Inspection and maintenance records** for the truck and trailer.
- **Dispatch and delivery records** that show the schedule the driver was under.

Some of this can be lost or overwritten. One of our first steps is a written demand that the company preserve it.

## Questions

### How long do I have to file an injury claim in Georgia?

For most injury claims, Georgia gives you **two years from the date of the injury** to file a lawsuit (O.C.G.A. § 9-3-33). Claims against a government are much shorter: written notice to a city is due within **six months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-33-5), and to a county or the State within **twelve months** (O.C.G.A. § 36-11-1 and § 50-21-26). Miss the deadline and the claim can be lost for good.

### What if I was partly at fault?

Georgia uses a modified comparative fault rule (O.C.G.A. § 51-12-33). You can still recover if you were less than 50% at fault, but your award is reduced by your share of the fault. At 50% or more, you recover nothing. Insurers know this, which is why they often try to shift blame onto the injured person.

### Will my case go to trial?

Many injury claims settle without a trial. Some don't, and insurers pay attention to which lawyers are ready to try a case. The firm has litigated in Georgia's state and federal courts, and Ben has tried cases to verdict.
